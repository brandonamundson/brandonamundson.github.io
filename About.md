Born and raised in Rapid City, South Dakota. I grew up using computers, my parents always had me playing educational games from a young age. In 4th grade, I grew to learn a lot about computers and how to use them even more. I was taught how to use programs such as Word and PowerPoint and I also managed to learn some keyboard shortcuts. In middle and high school, I was able to explore my passion for computers even more. Once I graduated from Rapid City Central High School, I attended the South Dakota School of Mines & Technology for a couple of years. I started my degree in Computer Engineering but realized that as much as I enjoyed learning about the hardware that makes computers work, I much preferred the operating system and programming side more and switched degrees to Computer Science.

I did not complete my degree at the South Dakota School of Mines & Technology, but instead took a break from school for two years working retail and as an office manager for my cousin's business, Reber Sanitation, before eventually deciding to finish my degree at the University of Advancing Technology in Tempe, AZ.

<h1 align="center"> COMMUNITY STATEMENT </h1>

While continuing my educational journey in the fields of Advancing Computer Science and Network Security, I have learned so much by participating in different educational events. While participating in the Collegiate Cyber Defense Competition, aka CCDC, I learned a lot about how to identify network attacks as well as how to defend networks from an attack in-progress. I also learned how to take inventory of all computers and running services for both employees and customers on a network. While the focus of these events is primarily network security based, I do learn a lot about shell scripting and exploit creation as a way of better securing the networks we are tasked with securing.
{: .indent}

During production studios, I worked to further my education in both fields by working on projects that greatly expanded my knowledge of programming and network security. I spent one semester working on a project that tied an SQL database programmed in C# with a front end web interface programmed in Angular JS. Working on this project helped me to expand my knowledge of programming languages as well as how to integrate two very different systems to work as a single end product. The next semester that I participated in production studios, I worked to improve my skills working with Open Source Intelligence (OSINT). While performing OSINT, I researched my findings and prepared reports to ensure what I found was remedied to prevent malicious actors from taking advantage of security leaks.
{: .indent}

For my innovation project Hack Me Not, I am utilizing a visual novel engine called Ren’Py to build a visual representation of a classic tech support scam and demonstrate to the elderly and other tech illiterate how to recognize signs of scams in a form of graphic novel/video game. This project was birthed out of the need to teach my grandmother how not to get scammed as she was always accepting these types of calls or making calls to these scammers when she thought her computer had been hacked and when her screen showed the fake popups, she would comply and call the scammer’s number. My grandmother was not the only member of my family to fall for these types of scams, but she was the most prevalent. In addition, while working for OfficeMax, I assisted with their tech support systems and saw many computers whose owners, had fallen for these types of scams.
